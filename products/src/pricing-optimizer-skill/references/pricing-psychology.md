# Pricing psychology - the behavioral science

## The left-digit effect

₹1,299 feels significantly cheaper than ₹1,300 despite being ₹1 difference.

**Why**: we read left-to-right, so "1,2" registers before "99". The brain rounds down.

**Apply**: end prices in 9 or 99 (₹499, ₹999, ₹1,499). Avoid round numbers (₹500, ₹1,000) unless signaling premium/luxury.

## Anchoring

The first price seen becomes the reference point for all subsequent prices.

**Examples**:
- "Was ₹1,999, now ₹1,499" - the ₹1,999 makes ₹1,499 feel like a deal
- "Premium plan ₹999/mo, Basic plan ₹499/mo" - ₹999 makes ₹499 feel cheap
- Showing a ₹5,000 product next to ₹500 product makes ₹500 feel affordable

**Rules**:
- "Was" price must be real (was your actual price recently, or comparable product's price)
- Never fake anchors - customers check, trust dies
- Anchor works best when the higher price is visible first

## Decoy effect (asymmetric dominance)

Offer 3 options where one is clearly inferior - it makes the middle option look best.

**Example**:
- Basic: ₹299 (limited features)
- Standard: ₹499 (good features) ← most people pick this
- Premium: ₹799 (all features + extras)

The Basic plan makes Standard look like good value. The Premium plan makes Standard look reasonable.

## Price-quality heuristic

People assume higher price = higher quality (especially for unfamiliar products).

**Implication**: don't race to bottom on price - it signals low quality. If your product is genuinely good, price it accordingly.

## Round number signaling

- ₹500, ₹1,000, ₹5,000 = signals premium, clean, confident
- ₹499, ₹999, ₹4,999 = signals value, deal, accessible

**When to use round numbers**: luxury products, B2B, services, premium positioning  
**When to use charm pricing**: mass market, impulse buys, value positioning

## The "pain of paying" principle

People feel loss more than they feel gain. Reduce perceived pain:

- **EMI display**: "₹250/month" feels smaller than "₹2,999"
- **Per-unit framing**: "₹5/day" feels cheaper than "₹1,500"
- **Free shipping threshold**: "Free shipping over ₹999" encourages higher cart value
- **Bundle savings**: "Save ₹200 when you buy together" frames as gain, not loss

## Social proof in pricing

- "10,000+ sold at this price" = validates the price point
- "Customers also bought" = anchors to similar products
- "Only X left at this price" = urgency without discounting
