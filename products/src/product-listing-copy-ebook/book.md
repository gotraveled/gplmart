# The Ecommerce Listing Copy Bible

**Write product listings that convert - titles, bullets, descriptions and keywords for Shopify, Amazon, Flipkart and Meesho.**

Most product listings fail for one reason: they describe the product instead of selling the outcome. This guide fixes that - with copy frameworks you can apply to any product, any platform, today.

---

## Chapter 1 - Why listings fail (and what actually works)

Walk through any marketplace and you'll see the same pattern: listings written by manufacturers, in spec-speak, addressed to nobody. "Premium quality stainless steel construction with advanced insulation technology." Technically true. Completely useless.

The buyer doesn't care about your construction. They care about their problem getting solved.

### The conversion equation

```
Listing converts = Relevance x Clarity x Trust x Desire
```

- **Relevance**: does this listing speak to MY situation?
- **Clarity**: can I understand what this is and why I need it in 5 seconds?
- **Trust**: do I believe the claims?
- **Desire**: do I want the outcome enough to buy now?

Most listings optimize for none of these. They optimize for "including all keywords" and call it SEO.

### The golden rule

Write for the buyer's inner monologue. When someone considers a product, they think in questions:

- "Will this actually work for me?"
- "Is it worth the price?"
- "What if it breaks/doesn't fit/doesn't work?"
- "Why should I buy from THIS seller?"

Your listing should answer these questions before they're asked.

## Chapter 2 - The title: your 5-second pitch

The title is the hardest-working real estate on any listing. On Amazon it determines search ranking AND click-through. On Shopify it's often the first thing seen.

### The formula

```
[Brand] [Product Type] [Key Feature/Material] [Size/Count] [Use Case]
```

### Examples by category

| Bad title | Good title | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "Lunch Box" | "Hiso HR Steel Lunch Box 3-Tier 1000ml Office tiffin" | Brand + type + material + key feature + use case |
| "Kurti for women" | "Women Cotton Straight Kurti Printed Daily Wear M" | Specific fabric + fit + occasion + size |
| "Earbuds" | "Boat Airdopes 141 TWS Earbuds 42H Playback ENC" | Brand + model + key specs buyers search for |
| "Face serum" | "Minimalist Vitamin C Serum 10% Face Glow 30ml" | Concern + ingredient + result + size |

### Platform limits

- **Amazon**: ~200 chars, no ALL CAPS, no promotional text, no symbols
- **Flipkart**: ~150 chars
- **Meesho**: ~100 chars (shorter is better - resellers rewrite anyway)
- **Shopify**: flexible, but SEO best practice is under 60 chars for the title tag

### Title dos and don'ts

DO: front-load searchable keywords, include the spec that differentiates you, write like a human  
DON'T: write "BEST QUALITY PREMIUM PRODUCT!!!", keyword-stuff, include "free shipping" (Amazon penalizes it)

## Chapter 3 - Bullet points: benefits that sell

Bullets are where browsers become buyers. Five bullets, each doing one job.

### The formula

```
[Benefit] + [specific feature/proof] + [why it matters to the buyer]
```

**Bad**: "Made of stainless steel"  
**Good**: "Keeps food hot for 5+ hours - triple-layer stainless steel insulation keeps tiffin warm till lunch"

### Per-category bullet patterns

**Kitchen/food**: taste or texture → ingredients → quantity → occasion  
**Fashion**: fit → fabric → occasion → care  
**Electronics**: headline spec → battery/connectivity → use case → warranty  
**Beauty**: skin concern → ingredient % → expected result → size  
**Home**: function → material → capacity → convenience

### Bullet rules

- Start each with ✓ or • - consistent symbol, never mix
- One idea per bullet - never stack three features in one line
- Front-load the benefit in the first 10 words (mobile truncates)
- Add proof where possible: "BPA-free", "IS:1762 certified", "tested for 500 washes"

## Chapter 4 - The description: where decisions happen

Short descriptions skim. Long descriptions convince. The description is where you handle objections, paint the use case and close the sale.

### Structure that works

```
[HOOK - 2-3 sentences addressing the core problem]

WHY [PRODUCT] - [expanded features with context]

PERFECT FOR: [who/when/where use cases]

SPECIFICATIONS:
[table format - scannable]

[TRUST: warranty, certifications, what's included]

FAQ: [3-5 real buyer questions answered]
```

### The hook matters most

First 2-3 sentences decide whether they keep reading. Write it for the buyer's situation:

Bad: "Introducing our new premium lunch box with advanced features."  
Good: "Tired of soggy rotis and cold sabzi by lunch? This 3-tier steel tiffin keeps everything hot for 5 hours."

### FAQ section = objection handling

Every FAQ is a buying objection you're answering before it's asked:

- "Will it fit in my bag?" → dimensions + weight
- "Is it durable?" → material + warranty
- "How do I clean it?" → care instructions
- "What if it breaks?" → return/replacement policy

## Chapter 5 - Keywords: being found without being spammy

Keywords get you into search results. Good copy gets you clicked. You need both.

### The free research method

1. **Search bar autocomplete**: type your product type on Amazon/Flipkart - suggestions are real buyer searches
2. **Competitor listings**: top 3 competitors - what words repeat in titles and bullets?
3. **People Also Ask**: Google your product - the questions become FAQ content and long-tail keywords
4. **Google Trends**: check if your terms are rising or dying

### Keyword placement

- **Primary keyword**: title + first bullet + description opening + URL handle
- **Secondary keywords**: scattered naturally through bullets and description
- **Long-tail**: FAQ answers and use-case paragraphs
- **Regional terms**: "tiffin dabba" alongside "lunch box" for Indian marketplaces

### Backend keywords

Amazon's hidden keyword field: 250 bytes, comma-separated, don't repeat title words. Use synonyms and alternate spellings buyers actually type.

### What kills rankings

- Keyword stuffing ("lunch box tiffin dabba steel box food container")
- Irrelevant popular keywords ("mobile phone" on a lunch box listing)
- Competitor brand names in your copy (trademark violation)

## Chapter 6 - Platform-specific playbook

### Amazon

- Title: strictly follow rules - no promo text, no symbols, no ALL CAPS
- Bullets: exactly 5, ~500 chars each, benefit-led
- Description: plain text works better than HTML
- Images matter MORE than copy on Amazon - great photos + decent copy beats great copy + bad photos

### Flipkart

- Shorter title (~150 chars) - punchy, keyword-front
- Emphasize Flipkart-specific: exchange offer, EMI, Plus subscription
- Category taxonomy matters - pick the most specific leaf category

### Meesho

- Audience = resellers + extreme value buyers
- Mention resale/margin potential - Meesho buyers think like resellers
- Shorter copy, mobile-first formatting wins
- WhatsApp/catalog angle resonates

### Shopify (own store)

- No character limits - write for conversion, not algorithm
- Use metafields for structured data (size guides, care instructions)
- Product description = brand voice showcase - storytelling welcome
- SEO title and meta description separate from display title

## Chapter 7 - The pre-publish checklist

Before any listing goes live:

**Accuracy**
- [ ] Every spec verified against supplier sheet or real product
- [ ] No invented materials, dimensions, certifications
- [ ] Warranty/returns match actual policy
- [ ] Price and offer claims are real

**Copy quality**
- [ ] Title under platform limit, keywords front-loaded
- [ ] Bullets benefit-first, one idea each
- [ ] Description answers: what, why, who, how, specs, objections
- [ ] Keywords natural, no stuffing
- [ ] Spell check done (typos kill trust instantly)

**Conversion**
- [ ] Hook addresses real buyer pain
- [ ] Social proof somewhere (reviews, numbers, "10,000+ sold")
- [ ] Clear call-to-action implied
- [ ] Mobile-readable: short paragraphs, scannable

## Chapter 8 - Scaling: writing 50 listings without losing quality

Once the system works for one product:

1. **Template everything**: title formula, bullet structure, description sections - fill in per product
2. **Batch research**: gather all supplier specs first, then write in batches
3. **Consistency check**: same product family = consistent voice, different products = varied hooks
4. **Review loop**: check which listings convert best, extract patterns, update templates
5. **Seasonal refresh**: update titles/bullets for festivals, sales, trends

The goal isn't 50 perfect listings - it's a system that produces 80% quality at scale, then you manually polish the top 20% that drive most revenue.
