# Finding differentiation - the gaps nobody owns

## The positioning map

Plot competitors on two axes:

```
Price ↑
      |  Premium niche    |  Luxury experience
      |  (few players)    |  (brand-driven)
------+-------------------+-------------------
      |  Budget volume    |  Mid-market value
      |  (crowded)        |  (most competition)
      +-------------------+-------------------
     Low differentiation → High differentiation
```

The gap = where few competitors sit but demand exists.

## Gap types to look for

**Price gap**: nobody serves the budget segment well (all ₹999+, you launch at ₹499)  
**Quality gap**: everyone sells cheap imports, nobody does quality control  
**Service gap**: everyone has 7-day returns + slow support, you offer 15-day + WhatsApp support  
**Niche gap**: everyone sells "general" products, you own a specific use case ("lunch boxes for diabetics")  
**Channel gap**: everyone is website-only, you do WhatsApp-first commerce  
**Content gap**: nobody educates their buyers, you become the category teacher

## Differentiation that actually works

1. **Niche down**: "lunch boxes" → "lunch boxes for office workers who care about health" - smaller market, zero competition
2. **Service superiority**: faster shipping, better returns, real human support - competitors can't copy service culture quickly
3. **Content authority**: become the go-to resource for your category - buyers choose the brand that taught them
4. **Community**: WhatsApp groups, UGC challenges, loyalty programs - competitors can copy features, not community
5. **Speed**: trend-jacking faster than competitors - they take 2 weeks to list a viral product, you take 2 days

## What NOT to differentiate on

- Price alone (race to bottom, no loyalty)
- "Better quality" without proof (every competitor claims this)
- More products (breadth ≠ differentiation, focus does)
- Generic "customer service" (table stakes, not differentiator)
