# Cover Letter Templates — 5 Situations

Cover letters are short (150–250 words), specific, and human. These templates cover the five situations freelancers actually face. Each is built around one job: **prove relevance fast, ask for one small next step.**

---

## 1. Cold Application (no prior contact, no portfolio piece sent yet)

**Use when:** Applying to a job post or reaching out cold to a company.

---

Hi [Name],

[HOOK — one specific, genuine observation about their business. Example: "I noticed [Company] just launched [product/feature] — congrats. I specialize in exactly the kind of [work] that kind of launch needs next: [specific thing]."]

I'm a [your role] with [X] years of experience helping [type of client] with [core skill]. Most recently, [one proof point with a number — "I cut [Client]'s page load time 52% and their conversion rose 18% in 60 days"].

What caught my eye about [Company] specifically is [why them — their product, values, a detail only someone who looked would mention]. I'd love to be the person who [outcome you want for them].

My portfolio: [link]
Resume: [link or attached]

Would you be open to a 15-minute call this week, or should I send over a quick audit of [their site/process] first?

Best,
[Your name]
[Your title/role]
[Portfolio link]

## 2. Referral Introduction

**Use when:** Someone referred you — the warmest cold email you'll ever send. The referrer's name does the heavy lifting; your job is to not waste it.

---

Hi [Name],

[Referrer's name] suggested I reach out — they thought my work on [type of project] could help with [Company]'s [specific need].

Quick background: I'm a [role] with [X] years in [field]. [One standout result with number — "For [similar client], I [did thing] which [result]"].

[Referrer] mentioned [specific thing they said — e.g., "you're rebuilding the onboarding flow" / "you need someone who can own paid social without a big team"]. That's squarely in my wheelhouse — in fact, [brief relevant example].

I've attached my portfolio ([link]) and resume. If it's useful, I'd love 15 minutes to hear what [Company] is trying to achieve — and I'll tell you honestly whether I'm the right fit or point you to someone better.

Thanks for the intro, [Referrer] — and [Name], I appreciate you taking a look.

Best,
[Your name]

---

*Pro tip: tell your referrer exactly what you're sending so they can give the recipient a one-line heads-up. Referrals convert 3–5x better with a warm intro call or email.*

## 3. After Portfolio Piece (following up on work you sent)

**Use when:** You've already sent samples/audit and want the meeting without being pushy.

---

Hi [Name],

Hope the [portfolio piece/audit/teardown] I sent over was useful — I'd love to hear your honest reaction, even if it's "not quite right."

Quick context on why I built it that way: [1–2 sentences showing you think strategically — e.g., "I focused on [X] because in my experience that's where [their type of business] loses the most money before the first click even happens"].

If the direction resonates, the natural next step would be [specific low-commitment action — "a 30-min walkthrough of the audit" / "a paid trial project on one piece"]. If not, no hard feelings — and if you know someone who'd benefit from this kind of work, I'd genuinely appreciate the intro.

Either way, thanks for considering me.

Best,
[Your name]

## 4. Responding to a Job Post

**Use when:** There's a specific posting and you want to stand out from the 50+ applicants.

---

Hi [Hiring manager name if known — otherwise "Hi [Company] team"],

I saw your post for [role] and it immediately stood out because [specific detail — e.g., "most posts say 'fast-paced environment'; yours actually describes the deliverables and the metrics you'll be judged on, which tells me you know what good looks like"].

I'm a [role] with [X] years doing exactly this for [client type]. Two things from your list I can prove:

- [Requirement 1] → [proof: "At [Client], I [did X], resulting in [metric]"]
- [Requirement 2] → [proof: "For [Client], I [did Y], which [result]"]

On [nice-to-have or interesting detail from the post], I [relevant experience — honest, even if partial].

I've included my portfolio ([link]) with [1–2 pieces most relevant to their needs]. I'd welcome a chance to discuss how I'd approach [their biggest stated challenge].

Best,
[Your name]
[Portfolio link]

## 5. Re-engaging a Past Client

**Use when:** You've done work for someone before (or they ghosted) and want to restart the relationship. Warmest channel you have.

---

Hi [Name],

It's been [timeframe] since we worked together on [project] — I was thinking about [specific thing: "how [result] has probably evolved" / "the [season/launch] coming up"].

Quick update on my end: I've been deep in [new skill/niche/result — e.g., "Shopify CRO work — my last three clients averaged +24% conversion in 60 days"], and it made me think of [Company]'s [relevant situation].

If [their previous need / new plausible need] is on your radar this [quarter/year], I'd love to catch up for 15 minutes — no pitch, just a conversation about what's changed and whether I can help.

And if the timing isn't right, totally understand — either way, good to reconnect.

Best,
[Your name]

---

*Variation for a past client who didn't convert:* reference what you learned ("When we talked last year, the blocker was X — has that changed?"). Shows you remember and gives them an easy yes/no.*

---

## Quick Rules for All Cover Letters

- **Under 250 words.** If it's longer, it's a proposal — and proposals come after they reply.
- **First sentence must be about them**, not you. "I'm a passionate…" is dead on arrival.
- **One ask only.** A call, a reply, or a sample — never "please review my attached resume and portfolio and let me know your thoughts on next steps."
- **No clichés:** "I hope this email finds you well," "I'm a hard worker," "synergy," "guru/ninja/rockstar."
- **Proof > adjectives, always.** One number beats three compliments.
- **Sign-off with links.** Portfolio and LinkedIn in the signature — make the next click effortless.
