# Resume & Freelance Proposal Kit — The Complete Guide

Most freelancers lose jobs not because they aren't good enough, but because their resume and proposal never get read properly — or get rejected in six seconds. This kit gives you six ready-to-fill resume variants, eight proposal templates, and five cover letters. This guide explains how to use them, how to tailor them for every client, and the tactics that turn a generic application into a reply.

---

## 1. How This Kit Works

Everything in this kit is plain Markdown. That is deliberate:

1. **Copy** any template you need.
2. **Fill in** the `[BRACKETED]` placeholders with your real details.
3. **Paste** it into Google Docs, Word, Notion, or Typora.
4. **Style it** (fonts, headings, PDF export) and save your master version.
5. **Tailor it** for each application using the checklist in Section 5.

Why Markdown? Because it forces clean structure. If your resume reads well as plain text, it will read beautifully as a PDF. No design skill required — structure does the heavy lifting.

## 2. Pick the Right Resume Variant

You have six resume templates. Each is optimized for a different buyer of your services. Choose based on *what the client thinks they are hiring*, not just what you do.

| File | Best for | Who hires you | What they scan for first |
|---|---|---|---|
| `resume-01-tech-general.md` | Developers, engineers, full-stack freelancers | Startups, agencies, tech founders | Tech stack, years, shipped projects |
| `resume-02-ecommerce-shopify.md` | Shopify / Liquid / theme developers | Store owners, ecom agencies | Store count, revenue impact, platform depth |
| `resume-03-digital-marketer-meta-ads.md` | Media buyers, growth marketers | DTC brands, agencies | ROAS, spend managed, CAC reduction |
| `resume-04-graphic-designer-brand.md` | Designers, brand identity specialists | Founders, small businesses | Visual range, brand system thinking |
| `resume-05-content-writer-copywriter.md` | Writers, copywriters, SEO content | SaaS, blogs, ecom brands | Niches, traffic/revenue results, samples |
| `resume-06-ecommerce-dropshipping-operator.md` | Store operators, dropshippers, ecom managers | Ecom entrepreneurs | Stores run, revenue, ad spend owned |

**Rule of thumb:** if a client can't guess your niche from the first three lines, you picked the wrong template or wrote a weak summary.

### When to use more than one

- **Shopify developer who also runs ads** → use `resume-02` for Shopify jobs, `resume-03` for marketing jobs. Never merge them.
- **Designer who codes landing pages** → use `resume-04` for brand work, `resume-01` for build work.
- Job posts are read by keyword. Matching the variant to the post's language gets you past the first filter.

## 3. The 6-Second Rule

Recruiters and busy founders spend about **six seconds** on a resume scan. In that time they look for:

1. **Your name and title** — "Shopify Developer," not "Freelancer."
2. **Contact + links** — can they reach you and see proof?
3. **The first bullet under Experience** — is it impressive?
4. **Numbers** — did you move anything?
5. **Recency** — are you active now?

Everything else is read only if you pass this gate.

### What kills a resume in six seconds

- Objective statements: "Seeking a challenging role where I can grow…" — nobody cares. Delete them.
- Paragraph-length summaries — write 2–3 lines max.
- Bullets without numbers — "Responsible for managing social media" is a chore description, not an achievement.
- Walls of skills with 40 items — list what you use daily, grouped.
- Outdated dates — a gap of more than 6 months needs a one-line explanation or a current project.
- Generic titles — "Marketing Manager at [Startup]" is fine; "Rockstar Ninja Guru" gets filed.

### The front-load test

Cover your resume except the top third. What do you see? You should see: name, title, location, contact, links, and the first line of your summary. If the most important info isn't there, restructure.

## 4. ATS + Humans: Write for Both

**ATS (Applicant Tracking Systems)** scan for keywords before a human ever sees your resume. Platforms like Upwork, LinkedIn, and most job boards use them. Humans then read the survivors.

### ATS rules

- **Use standard section headers**: `Experience`, `Education`, `Skills`, `Projects`. Weird headers like "Where I've Made Magic Happen" get skipped.
- **Mirror the job post's keywords naturally** — if they say "Shopify Liquid," don't write only "theme customization." Both terms can appear.
- **No text in images or tables** — ATS can't read them. Keep it plain.
- **Standard fonts and no columns** in the final PDF.
- **File name**: `Firstname-Lastname-Shopify-Developer.pdf`, not `resume_final_v3(2).pdf`.

### Human rules

- **Write like you talk, but tighter.** First person is fine in a summary ("I build…"); bullets stay implied-first-person with strong verbs.
- **One page for <10 years experience.** Two pages max. Nobody reads page three.
- **White space is credibility.** Breathing room signals a senior operator.
- **Proof over adjectives.** "Fast, reliable, detail-oriented" = zero information. "Shipped 12 Shopify migrations with zero downtime" = proof.

## 5. Tailoring Checklist (Do This Every Time)

Tailoring is not rewriting — it's a 15-minute pass:

1. **Read the job post twice.** Highlight repeated words and phrases. Those are the keywords.
2. **Pick the matching resume variant.** Adjust the professional summary's first sentence to echo the client's goal.
3. **Reorder Experience bullets.** Put the most relevant achievement first under each role.
4. **Swap one or two skills.** Add the exact tool they mentioned if you genuinely know it.
5. **Mirror the niche.** Applying to a skincare brand? Mention beauty/wellness clients if you have them. If you don't, pick the closest adjacent niche — don't lie, but frame honestly.
6. **Update the links.** Link the most relevant portfolio piece at the top.
7. **Name the file** for the role and send a PDF, not .md.
8. **Pair it with a tailored proposal** from `proposals.md` — the resume supports the proposal, it doesn't replace it.

## 6. Quantifying Achievements: The Formula

Weak bullet: *"Improved website performance."*

Strong bullet: *"Reduced page load time from 4.2s to 1.1s by lazy-loading images and optimizing Liquid renders — conversions rose 18%."*

### The formula

> **Strong action verb + what you did + measurable result + method/context**

### Action verbs that signal ownership

Built, Shipped, Launched, Scaled, Reduced, Increased, Generated, Migrated, Automated, Negotiated, Restructured, Grew, Cut, Converted, Delivered

Avoid: Responsible for, Helped with, Assisted in, Participated in, Worked on.

### Before/after examples

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| Managed client's social media accounts | Grew Instagram engagement 210% in 6 months by shifting to short-form video and a test-and-iterate content calendar |
| Did Shopify theme customization | Built 14 custom Shopify sections in Liquid, cutting the client's dev costs by $3,200/yr vs. agency retainer |
| Handled Meta ads | Cut CPA from $38 to $21 while scaling spend from $10k to $40k/month across 6 ad accounts |
| Wrote blog posts for clients | Published 48 SEO articles driving 92k organic visits/mo; two posts ranked #1 for target keywords |
| Designed logos for businesses | Delivered 30+ brand identities; 12 clients returned for full website design within 90 days |
| Helped with dropshipping store | Ran a 3-store portfolio generating $61k/mo; killed 2 underperformers, scaled winner to $38k/mo |

### When you don't have numbers

- **Scale**: "across 3 stores," "for a 200k-follower brand"
- **Speed**: "in 11 days," "2 weeks ahead of deadline"
- **Scope**: "full rebrand — logo, packaging, site"
- **Quality proxies**: "zero refund requests," "zero downtime migrations," "featured in [publication]"

Never invent numbers. If a metric is confidential, use ranges ("~$30k/mo") or ask the client for permission to share.

## 7. The Proposal That Gets Replies

A proposal is not a cover letter and not a pitch deck. It's a **mini-audit** that proves you understood the client's problem before they hire you.

### The winning structure: Context → Relevance → Proof → CTA

**1. Subject line** — specific, not clever.
- Bad: "Proposal for your project"
- Good: "Shopify rebuild for [Brand]: keeping your best-converting PDPs, fixing the rest"

**2. Opening hook (1–2 lines)** — show you did homework.
Reference something specific about their business: a product, a competitor, a recent launch, a gap you noticed.

**3. Understanding of need (1 short paragraph)** — reflect their goal back in their words. This is where most freelancers fail: they talk about themselves instead of the client's problem.

**4. Relevance (3–5 bullets)** — why *you* specifically can solve *this* problem. Mirror their keywords. One line each, proof-forward.

**5. Scope/approach outline** — a numbered 4–6 step plan. Shows you've done this before.

**6. Timeline** — give a range ("3–4 weeks"), not a promise ("I will finish in exactly 21 days").

**7. Pricing anchor** — give a range or "starting at." Anchoring matters (Section 8).

**8. CTA** — one clear next step. "Want me to send a quick Loom walkthrough of your current funnel?" Low-friction beats "Let's hop on a call."

### What gets proposals deleted instantly

- Walls of text with no structure
- "Dear Hiring Manager" on Upwork (you know their name)
- Generic templates with unfilled placeholders — the #1 trust killer
- Questions that should be obvious ("What is your budget?") before showing value
- Over-apologizing or underselling ("I know I'm not the best, but…")

## 8. Rate Anchoring: How to Price Without Losing

**Anchoring** = the first number mentioned frames everything after it.

### Rules

- **Never be the cheapest option** — cheap attracts bad clients and trains the market to devalue you.
- **Give a range, not a single number**, when you must quote early: "$2,500–$4,000 depending on scope." The top of the range is your anchor.
- **Quote value, then price.** "This typically recovers its cost in 6–8 weeks of improved conversion" makes $4k feel small.
- **Hourly vs. project**: prefer project pricing for defined deliverables. Hourly invites micromanagement. If hourly, set a minimum engagement ("10 hrs/mo minimum").
- **Deposits**: 30–50% upfront, milestone-based for anything over ~$1k. Non-negotiable.
- **Raise rates every 3–6 months** or when demand exceeds capacity. Grandfather existing clients for 60 days.

### Platform benchmarks (directional, adjust for your market)

| Role | Entry | Mid | Senior |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shopify dev | $35–50/hr | $60–100/hr | $120+/hr or $5k+ per build |
| Meta ads mgmt | $500–800/mo + ad spend | $1,000–2,500/mo | $3k+/mo or % of spend |
| Graphic design | $30–60/hr | $75–150/hr | project-based brand systems |
| Content writing | $0.10–0.20/word | $0.25–0.50/word | retainers $1.5k+/mo |
| Virtual assistant | $8–15/hr | $15–30/hr | $30+/hr specialized |
| Video editing | $25–60/hr | $60–120/hr | per-video or channel retainers |

*US/EU markets sit higher; adjust honestly for your region and never race to the bottom.*

## 9. Follow-Up Cadence: Where Replies Actually Come From

**~80% of freelance work goes to the 2nd, 3rd, or 4th follow-up** — the ones most freelancers never send.

### The cadence

| Day | Action |
|---|---|
| Day 0 | Send tailored proposal. Log it. |
| Day 3 | Short bump: add one new relevant proof point or insight about their business. |
| Day 7 | Value-add follow-up: send a teardown, audit, or Loom of their current setup (free sample of your work). |
| Day 14 | Polite close: "I'll assume timing isn't right — want me to keep you in mind for Q[X]?" This often triggers the reply. |
| Day 30+ | Re-engage quarterly with something new (see cover letter #5). |

### Follow-up rules

- Each follow-up must **add something new** — a result, an observation, a resource. "Just checking in!" is spam.
- One follow-up per channel per week max.
- Never apologize for following up. You're a professional following a professional process.
- Track everything in a simple sheet: client, date sent, follow-up dates, status, notes.

### The free-sample follow-up (most powerful)

Instead of "just bumping this," do 20 minutes of work: audit their Shopify speed, teardown their ad account structure, critique their homepage hero. Send it as a Loom or short doc. Even if they don't hire you, you've demonstrated competence no proposal can match — and often they'll hire *you* because you already started.

## 10. Platform-Specific Tips

### Upwork

- **Profile = resume.** Optimize it with the same keyword discipline. Your title matters: "Shopify Liquid Developer | Theme Customization & Speed Optimization."
- **First 2 lines of a proposal** are all most clients read. Put the hook there.
- **Boosted proposals** (paid) get ~2–3x visibility on competitive jobs — worth it for high-value posts.
- **Connects cost money** — don't spray. Apply to fewer, better-fit jobs with tailored proposals. Win rate beats volume.
- **JSS (Job Success Score)** matters for Top Rated. Over-deliver on small jobs early.

### LinkedIn

- Your LinkedIn "Featured" section should hold your best 2–3 portfolio pieces.
- Post 2–3x/week about your niche — clients check your activity before replying.
- InMail/InMail-style outreach: reference a specific post or project of theirs.
- "Open to Work" for services is fine, but better is a services page + portfolio link in your bio.

### Direct outreach (email / cold DM)

- The highest-converting channel for experienced freelancers — zero platform fees, zero competition.
- Personalize the first line using something you genuinely found (their new product launch, a bad review, a competitor's campaign).
- Keep cold emails under 150 words. One ask: a 15-minute call or a yes/no on a paid mini-audit.
- Tools: Hunter.io for emails, LinkedIn Sales Navigator trial, Google Alerts for funding/launch news in your niche.

### Marketplaces beyond Upwork

- **Shopify Experts marketplace** — apply; clients there arrive pre-qualified.
- **Contra, Twine, Fiverr Pro** — lower fees/curated tiers worth testing.
- **Niche communities** — Shopify Community, r/shopify, design Slack groups, ecom Discord servers. Give value first; DMs convert later.

## 11. Resume + Proposal Pairing Rules

1. **The proposal leads; the resume supports.** Attach the resume (or link it) — don't make the client dig.
2. **Never send identical pairs.** If your resume and proposal are both generic, you're invisible.
3. **On Upwork**, proposals often substitute for resumes — put your strongest 3 bullets inside the proposal itself.
4. **Keep a master resume** per variant, and a "submission" version per application. Track which version won.
5. **Update monthly** — add one new project or result. Stale resumes lose to fresh ones.

## 12. Common Mistakes Checklist

- [ ] Sending the same resume for every application
- [ ] Generic objective statement instead of a value summary
- [ ] Bullets with no numbers
- [ ] Skills section stuffed with everything you've ever touched
- [ ] No portfolio link anywhere
- [ ] Proposal that talks about you for 3 paragraphs before mentioning their problem
- [ ] No follow-up after day 0
- [ ] Quoting a single low price to "get the job"
- [ ] Leaving placeholders like [Your Name] in a sent document
- [ ] Applying to jobs you can't actually do — reputation compounds faster than income

## 13. Your 7-Day Action Plan

**Day 1:** Pick your variant. Fill in header, summary, and skills. Don't perfect it.
**Day 2:** Write Experience bullets using the formula. Aim for 3 roles × 4 bullets.
**Day 3:** Add Projects + Education. Export PDF. Send to one trusted person for feedback.
**Day 4:** Customize `proposals.md` — pick your 3 most common job types and fill them fully.
**Day 5:** Apply to 3 real jobs with fully tailored resume + proposal pairs.
**Day 6:** Set follow-up reminders for days 3, 7, 14.
**Day 7:** Review what got replies vs. silence. Adjust hooks and proof points accordingly.
