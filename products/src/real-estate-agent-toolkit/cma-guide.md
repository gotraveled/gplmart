# Comparative Market Analysis (CMA) Guide

A step-by-step guide to pulling comps, adjusting for differences, and setting a price that sells. The CMA is the foundation of every listing presentation — get it right and the rest follows.

> **Core principle:** A CMA isn't about finding the "right" price — it's about finding the price that maximizes your client's net proceeds while ensuring the home sells in a reasonable timeframe. Price too high and it sits; price too low and you leave money on the table.

---

## 1. How to Pull Comps

### 1.1 Define Your Search Parameters

Before you start pulling data, define your search criteria:

| Parameter | Your Criteria | Notes |
|-----------|---------------|-------|
| Radius | ___ miles | Start tight (0.5 mi), expand if needed |
| Timeframe | Last ___ days | 90 days for active markets, 180 for slower |
| Property type | Single-family / Condo / Townhome | Must match subject |
| Bedrooms | ___ beds | ±1 bedroom max |
| Bathrooms | ___ baths | ±1 bathroom max |
| Square footage | ___ – ___ sqft | ±20% of subject |
| Lot size | ___ – ___ sqft | ±25% of subject |
| Year built | ___ – ___ | ±10 years of subject |
| Condition | Similar condition | Updated, dated, fixer |

### 1.2 Pull Three Types of Comps

| Type | What It Is | Why It Matters |
|------|------------|----------------|
| **Sold comps** | Recently closed sales | Most reliable indicator of market value |
| **Active listings** | Currently on market | Shows competition and pricing |
| **Expired/withdrawn** | Failed to sell | Shows what doesn't work |

### 1.3 Data Sources

- **MLS** (primary source) — sold, active, expired listings
- **Public records** — county assessor, recorder's office
- **Zillow/Redfin/Realtor.com** — supplemental data, but verify with MLS
- **Appraiser data** — if available, for additional context

## 2. Adjustment Factors

No two homes are identical. Adjust for differences to get an apples-to-apples comparison.

### 2.1 Common Adjustment Factors

| Factor | How to Adjust | Typical Range | Example |
|--------|---------------|---------------|---------|
| **Condition** | $10–30 per sqft | Updated vs. dated | +$15,000 for updated kitchen |
| **Square footage** | $50–200 per sqft | Above/below average | +$75,000 for +1,000 sqft |
| **Lot size** | $1–5 per sqft | Above/below average | +$25,000 for +5,000 sqft lot |
| **Garage** | $5,000–$15,000 | 1-car vs. 2-car vs. 3-car | +$10,000 for 2-car garage |
| **Pool** | $5,000–$25,000 | In-ground vs. above-ground | +$15,000 for in-ground pool |
| **Age** | $5–$20 per sqft | Newer vs. older | +$10,000 for 10 years newer |
| **View** | $10,000–$50,000+ | Premium views | +$30,000 for water view |
| **Fireplace** | $3,000–$10,000 | Wood-burning vs. gas | +$5,000 for wood-burning |
| **Basement** | $5,000–$20,000 | Finished vs. unfinished | +$15,000 for finished basement |
| **Roof** | $5,000–$15,000 | New vs. old | +$10,000 for new roof |
| **HVAC** | $3,000–$10,000 | New vs. old | +$5,000 for new HVAC |
| **Updates** | Varies | Kitchen, bath, flooring | +$20,000 for kitchen remodel |

### 2.2 How to Apply Adjustments

1. **Start with the comp's sale price**
2. **Adjust for each difference** (add/subtract based on subject vs. comp)
3. **Sum the adjustments** to get adjusted price
4. **Average the adjusted prices** of all comps

### 2.3 Example Adjustment

| Comp | Sale Price | SqFt | Condition | Lot | Garage | Adjusted Price |
|------|------------|------|-----------|-----|--------|----------------|
| 1 | $400,000 | 2,000 | Updated | 7,500 sqft | 2-car | $400,000 |
| 2 | $380,000 | 1,800 | Dated | 6,000 sqft | 1-car | $395,000 |
| 3 | $420,000 | 2,200 | Updated | 8,000 sqft | 2-car | $410,000 |

**Subject:** 2,000 sqft, Updated, 7,500 sqft lot, 2-car garage

**Adjustments:**
- Comp 1: No adjustments needed (most similar)
- Comp 2: +$15,000 (updated vs. dated), +$20,000 (larger lot), +$10,000 (2-car vs. 1-car) = +$45,000 → $425,000
- Comp 3: -$10,000 (smaller than comp), -$5,000 (smaller lot) = -$15,000 → $405,000

**Average adjusted price:** ($400,000 + $425,000 + $405,000) / 3 = $410,000

## 4. Listing Appointment Script

### 4.1 Opening (First 5 Minutes)

> "Thanks for having me. Before we look at numbers, I'd like to spend 5 minutes understanding your goals — timeline, what matters most to you — and then I'll show you exactly what the market data says. Fair?"

### 4.2 Discovery Questions

1. Why are you selling now?
2. What's your ideal timeline?
3. Have you listed before? What worked/didn't work?
4. What's the minimum you'd accept, and what would be a dream outcome?
5. Are there any repairs or items you know need attention?

### 4.3 CMA Presentation

> "Based on [X] comparable sales in the last [Y] days within [Z] miles, homes in this [price range/condition tier] are averaging [DOM] days on market and selling at [X%] of list price. Your home at [address] is positioned as [aggressive/market/premium] because [specific reason]."

### 4.4 Pricing Discussion

> "The data points to [X]. Pricing at [market price] gets you [X] qualified buyers in the first two weekends and protects your negotiating position. Pricing higher to 'test the market' usually costs you [X] weeks and ends with a price cut anyway. Which scenario feels right to you?"

### 4.5 Marketing Plan

> "Here's my marketing plan: [list specific deliverables]. I'll handle [X], [Y], [Z]. You'll need to [seller responsibilities]."

### 4.6 Commission & Terms

> "My commission is [X]%, which works out to about $[Y] on a $[Z] sale. In exchange, you get [specific deliverables]. Am I worth $[Y]?"

### 4.7 Close

> "Based on everything we've discussed, I believe the best path forward is [recommended price] with [marketing plan]. I'm confident this will get you [expected outcome] in [timeline]. Are you ready to move forward, or is there anything else you'd like to discuss?"

## 5. Common CMA Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It's Wrong | How to Avoid |
|---------|---------------|--------------|
| **Using comps that are too old** | Market changes fast | Use comps within 90 days max |
| **Ignoring condition differences** | Updated vs. dated is a big factor | Adjust for condition explicitly |
| **Not adjusting for lot size** | Larger lots sell for more | Adjust for lot size |
| **Ignoring location factors** | School district, traffic, views matter | Factor in location premiums |
| **Over-relying on Zillow/Redfin** | Not always accurate | Verify with MLS data |
| **Not considering expired listings** | Shows what doesn't work | Include expired/withdrawn comps |
| **Pricing based on emotions** | Seller attachment ≠ market value | Use data, not feelings |
| **Not accounting for selling costs** | Net proceeds matter more than sale price | Always show net sheet |

---

> **KEY INSIGHT:** The CMA is a tool, not a crystal ball. It gives you a range, not a guarantee. The best agents use CMAs to start a conversation about pricing, not to dictate it. Your job is to present the data and help the seller make an informed decision.

> **PRO TIP:** Always bring printed CMAs to the listing appointment. Sellers trust data they can see and touch. Digital-only presentations feel less concrete and less credible.
