# Listing Presentation — Complete Fill-in Template

A ready-to-use framework for winning a seller's business at the listing appointment. Print this, fill in every bracketed field with real data from your CMA, and rehearse the script until it sounds like you — not a script.

> **How to use:** Each section mirrors the actual flow of a listing appointment. Sections marked **[FILL IN]** need your market data. Sections marked **[SCRIPT]** are talking points — deliver them in your own words. Time budget for a typical appointment: 45–60 minutes.

---

## 1. Pre-Listing Prep Checklist

Complete these steps **before** the appointment, not during it.

| # | Task | Done | Notes |
|---|------|------|-------|

## 2. Appointment Opening (First 5 Minutes)

**[SCRIPT]** Goal: control the agenda and build rapport before price is ever discussed.

> "Thanks for having me. Before we look at numbers, I'd like to spend 5 minutes understanding your goals — timeline, what matters most to you — and then I'll show you exactly what the market data says. Fair?"

**[FILL IN] Discovery questions to ask:**

1. Why are you selling now? (relocation, upsizing, downsizing, life event)
2. What's your ideal timeline?
3. Have you listed before? What worked / didn't work?
4. What's the minimum you'd accept, and what would be a dream outcome?
5. Are there any repairs or items you know need attention?
6. How involved do you want to be in showings and decisions?

| Showing instructions | Lockbox ☐ Yes / ☐ No-show ☐ Agent-present only |
| Seller concessions offered | $__________ toward buyer closing costs (if any) |

**[SCRIPT] Commission defense:**

> "My commission is [X]%, which works out to about $[Y] on a $[Z] sale. In exchange, you get [specific deliverables: professional photos, 3D tour, weekly reporting, negotiation expertise, transaction coordination]. On the buy side, my clients typically save [X] times my fee through negotiation and pricing strategy. Am I worth $[Y]?"

> **KEY INSIGHT:** Never apologize for commission. Frame it as an investment with a measurable return. If the seller pushes back, offer to adjust scope (e.g., 'I can reduce the marketing spend, but I can't reduce my time negotiating your largest financial transaction').

## 6. Objection-Handling Talking Points (15+)

### Objection 1: "Your commission is too high."

**Response:** "I understand budget matters. Let's look at what [X]% actually buys: professional marketing that gets [X] qualified showings, negotiation that could save you [X]% on the sale price, and transaction management so nothing falls through. On a $400,000 sale, a 1% difference is $4,000 — but a bad negotiation or a missed contingency can cost you $20,000. I'm not the cheapest option, but I'm the one who's closed [X] homes in this neighborhood."

### Objection 2: "We want to try FSBO first."

**Response:** "That's your right, and I respect it. I will tell you honestly: FSBO sellers in this market typically net [X]% less because they don't have access to MLS, professional photography, or negotiation leverage. If you try it for [30/60] days and it doesn't work, call me — I won't hold it against you. But know that every week on market costs you money in carrying costs and price reductions."

### Objection 3: "The market is slow; maybe we should wait."

**Response:** "Waiting feels safe, but it's usually expensive. Every month you wait costs you [mortgage payment + taxes + insurance + maintenance] ≈ $[X]. If prices are flat or declining [X]% per month, you're losing [Y] while you wait. And if the market turns up, you'll wish you had listed. The question isn't 'should we wait?' — it's 'what's the cost of waiting vs. the cost of listing now?'"

### Objection 4: "We need to do more repairs before listing."

**Response:** "I get it — you want to maximize value. But here's the data: homes that list with visible deferred maintenance sell for [X]% less AND sit [Y] days longer. The buyer will either walk away or demand a credit that exceeds the repair cost. Better to fix the big-ticket items (roof, HVAC, foundation) and disclose the cosmetic stuff, or price accordingly. Let's prioritize: what repairs give us the best ROI?"

### Objection 5: "We want to price high and see what happens."

**Response:** "I hear this a lot, and I want to be honest with you: the data says it almost never works. Homes that start [X]% above market typically sit for [Y] days, then get a price cut, then sell for [Z]% of list — lower than if they'd priced correctly from day one. Buyers see the price cut and assume something is wrong. The first two weekends are your best shot; don't waste them testing the market."

### Objection 6: "Can you reduce your commission if we sign today?"

**Response:** "My rate is [X]%, and it reflects the work I do: [list specific deliverables]. I can tell you that [Y]% of my business comes from referrals and repeat clients, which means my incentive is your success, not just this transaction. If you need to discuss budget, we can look at scope — maybe you handle staging yourself, or we adjust the marketing plan. But the core service stays the same."

### Objection 7: "We already have an agent we used before."

**Response:** "That's great — loyalty is valuable. May I ask what made them great, and what could have been better? I'm not asking you to leave them; I'm asking you to compare. If they're the best fit, I'll tell you. But if you have even 10% doubt, that's worth a conversation. What's the one thing you'd want to be different this time?"

### Objection 8: "We're not in a hurry; we'll list when the time is right."

**Response:** "I respect that, but 'when the time is right' is often 'when it's too late.' Markets shift fast, and the best time to sell is when demand is high and inventory is low. If we wait for perfect conditions, we may miss them. Let's set a timeline: if we're not listed by [date], what's the cost? Let's make a plan that protects your interests either way."

### Objection 9: "We don't want to deal with strangers in our house."

**Response:** "Totally valid concern. We can set up a lockbox with showing instructions, require pre-qualification for showings, or even limit showings to specific windows. You can also choose to be present or absent during showings — whatever makes you comfortable. My job is to make this process as easy as possible while still getting your home in front of qualified buyers."

### Objection 10: "What if the buyer's financing falls through?"

**Response:** "That's what contingencies are for. We'll include financing, appraisal, and inspection contingencies to protect you. If financing falls through, we negotiate repairs, price adjustments, or we relist. My job is to make sure you're not left holding the bag. We've handled [X] transactions; [Y] had financing issues, and every one was resolved."

### Objection 11: "We want to list on our own terms."

**Response:** "You absolutely can set terms — price, showing schedule, inclusions/exclusions. What you can't control is the buyer's side of the negotiation. I can help you set terms that attract qualified buyers while protecting your interests. The listing agreement is a contract, but it's also a framework for how we work together. Let's draft terms that work for both of us."

### Objection 12: "We think our home is worth more than the comps."

**Response:** "I understand the emotional attachment — your home is your biggest investment. But the market doesn't pay for memories; it pays for comparable sales. If your home has unique features that justify a premium, let's document them and find comps that support it. If not, we need to price based on what buyers will pay, not what you need. Let's look at the data together and see where the gap is."

### Objection 13: "We don't want to pay for staging."

**Response:** "Staging is optional, but the data is clear: staged homes sell [X]% faster and for [Y]% more. If budget is a concern, we can do a consultation-only approach — I'll walk through and tell you exactly what to move, what to remove, and what to add. Even $500 in minor changes can return $5,000 in sale price. Let's see what's feasible."

### Objection 14: "We're worried about the inspection."

**Response:** "Inspections are standard and protect everyone. If the inspector finds issues, we have options: negotiate repairs, request credits, or adjust the price. Most issues are minor and fixable. I'll be there during the inspection to answer questions and help you understand what's normal vs. what needs attention. You won't be alone in this."

### Objection 15: "We want to wait until the spring market."

**Response:** "Spring is great, but so is now. Inventory is [low/high], and buyers are [active/hesitant]. If we list now, we have less competition and more negotiating power. If we wait, we may face more competition and lower prices. Let's look at the seasonal data and see what makes sense for your timeline."

### Objection 16: "We want to list with a discount brokerage."

**Response:** "Discount brokerages offer less service for less money, which can work if you're experienced and have time. But if this is your biggest financial transaction, you want someone who's done this [X] times, knows the market, and will be there when things get complicated. Let's compare what you get for [X]% vs. [Y]% — the difference might be worth it."

### Objection 17: "We need to think about it."

**Response:** "Of course — this is a big decision. Take the time you need. But know that the longer you wait, the more the market may change. Let's set a follow-up date — say, [day/date] — and I'll check in. If you have questions before then, call me anytime. I'm here to help you make the best decision, not pressure you."

## 7. Close-the-Listing Script

**[SCRIPT] — Deliver when the seller signals readiness (or after you've addressed all objections).**

> "Based on everything we've discussed, I believe the best path forward is [recommended price] with [marketing plan]. I'm confident this will get you [expected outcome] in [timeline]. Are you ready to move forward, or is there anything else you'd like to discuss?"

**If yes:**

> "Great. Let's review the listing agreement together. [Walk through terms.] I'll need [X] documents: proof of ownership, mortgage payoff info, HOA docs if applicable, and your ID. Once we sign, I'll get the listing live within [24/48] hours. Any questions before we sign?"

**If hesitation:**

> "I understand. Let's schedule a follow-up call for [date/time] to address any remaining concerns. In the meantime, I'll send you the CMA and net sheet so you can review the numbers. I'm committed to getting this right for you."

**If they choose another agent:**

> "I respect your decision. If anything changes or you have questions, I'm here. I wish you the best, and I hope we can work together in the future — whether that's as your agent or as a referral partner."

## 8. Post-Appointment Follow-Up

| When | Action |
|------|--------|
| Same day | Send thank-you email with CMA, net sheet, marketing plan attached |
| Within 24 hours | Call to answer questions, address concerns |
| Day 3 | Check in: "Any questions? Ready to move forward?" |
| Day 7 | Final follow-up: "I'm here if you need me. Let's reconnect in [X] days." |
| If no response | Add to sphere-of-influence nurture campaign (see `lead-followup.md`) |

> **PRO TIP:** The listing appointment doesn't end when you leave. The follow-up is where deals are won or lost. Most agents give up after one call; the ones who close are the ones who follow up 5–7 times with value, not pressure.

## 9. Quick Reference: Listing Appointment Flow

```
1. Pre-listing prep (24–48 hrs before)
2. Arrival & rapport (5 min)
3. Discovery questions (10 min)
4. CMA presentation (15 min)
5. Pricing strategy discussion (10 min)
6. Marketing plan presentation (10 min)
7. Commission & terms (5 min)
8. Objection handling (10–15 min)
9. Close & next steps (5 min)
10. Post-appointment follow-up (ongoing)
```

**Total time:** 60–75 minutes. **Target close rate:** 60–70% for prepared agents.
